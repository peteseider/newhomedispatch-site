What a permanent rate buydown actually costs — and how to use that

A permanent below-market rate isn't free. To hold, say, 3.99% when the market is 6.66%, the builder pre-pays the lender a forward-commitment fee that commonly runs about 4–6% of the loan amount — roughly $18,000–$27,000 on a $450,000 loan. That is real builder money, and it is the same pool of money a builder could otherwise hand you as a price cut or closing-cost credit.

So the rate is a choice, not a gift. If you plan to keep the loan a long time, the permanent buydown usually wins. But if you're paying cash, expect to move or refinance within a few years, or simply value price over payment, ask the builder to reallocate that buydown cost to the sale price or to closing costs instead.

Today's sheet is a live example of why that ask matters: two builders that were offering a verified permanent rate as recently as yesterday (Perry Homes, and functionally Stylecraft Homes) are no longer confirmable today. Offers built around a rate promise can disappear or go stale without notice — confirm any rate or credit is still inside its stated window with a sales rep before you rely on the webpage alone, in either direction.

Hays County lands $39M for a railroad overpass on the Kyle growth corridor

The Federal Railroad Administration awarded Hays County a $39.23 million grant — 80% of project cost — to build a grade-separated overpass over the Union Pacific crossing at Centerpoint Road, running from I-35 to FM 2439/Hunter Road. Hays County and Union Pacific will cover the remaining ~$9.81 million. Preliminary engineering and schematic design are done; right-of-way acquisition and final design are underway, with no construction start date given yet. County officials pointed to faster, safer emergency-response routing as a key driver. (Community Impact, published today, Sept 1, 2026.)

For buyers: Centerpoint Road sits in the Kyle/Hays County growth corridor this sweep tracks closely — home to active communities from several builders on this sheet. This is new public investment in the corridor's road capacity and safety, but it's still in the design/right-of-way phase, so it won't shorten today's at-grade crossing waits. If you're evaluating a community near Centerpoint Road, I-35, or FM 2439, ask the builder or the county for the project's expected construction timeline before assuming near-term relief.
