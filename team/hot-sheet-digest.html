What a permanent rate buydown actually costs — and how to use that

A permanent below-market rate isn't free. To hold, say, 4.99% when the market is 6.95%, the builder pre-pays the lender a forward-commitment fee that commonly runs about 4–6% of the loan amount — roughly $18,000–$27,000 on a $450,000 loan. That is real builder money, and it is the same pool of money a builder could otherwise hand you as a price cut or closing-cost credit.

So the rate is a choice, not a gift. If you plan to keep the loan a long time, the permanent buydown usually wins. But if you're paying cash, expect to move or refinance within a few years, or simply value price over payment, ask the builder to reallocate that buydown cost to the sale price or to closing costs instead.

Today's rate move is a useful reminder that these permanent-rate offers aren't static: Risewell Homes raised its headline rate by a full point in step with the wider market. If you're comparing an offer you saw last week to what's live today, always re-check the current rate before assuming yesterday's numbers still apply.

Much of the funding traces to the 2022 Kyle Road Bond. (Community Impact, published Aug 27, 2026; re-checked Sept 18 — no fresher qualifying story found this run.)

For buyers: both Kyle and San Marcos sit inside the corridor this sweep tracks closely. Expect construction-related traffic and access disruptions near Center Street and Canyon Road into 2027 — but the bond-funded investment signals continued public support for road capacity in the corridor. If you're evaluating a community near either project, ask the builder or the county for the current construction timeline and detour routes before assuming smooth access.
