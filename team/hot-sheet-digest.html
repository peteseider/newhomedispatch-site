What Moved Today

  • Builders with live offers fell to 33 (from 36). Not a market pullback: four builders — Lennar, Starlight Homes, Saratoga Homes, and First America Homes — reverted to none-found on generic, unquantified page language, a pattern that has recurred repeatedly since November 6. Brohn Homes returned to the live board with two named Flex Cash percentage tiers, a genuine recovery.
  • Publishable dollar-bearing offers rose to 78 (from 67). Mostly a capture-granularity artifact: Coventry Homes was itemized to 6 per-listing rows (versus 2 summary rows yesterday) and Flintrock Builders to 7 per-community rows (versus 2) — deeper itemization of the same underlying sales events, not new incentive activity.
  • Blackburn Homes' price-cut listings demoted to leads for a 5th consecutive day. Struck-through list prices carry only generic status labels ("Available Now," "Reduced Pricing") with no dollar-incentive banner language, so they do not meet the publishable bar. Only the builder's genuine Flex Cash banner (Kyle) remains publishable.
  • Freddie Mac's 30-year fixed benchmark held at 7.11% for a 5th consecutive weekly reading (print dated Nov 12).

Corridor Coverage

13 of 19 priority builders in the Austin/Central Texas corridor were independently confirmed live at their own designated URL today, the same composition as yesterday. Taylor Morrison's page failed to load on direct retry for the 34th-plus consecutive run and remains unconfirmed; this is tracked as a fetch failure, not evidence the builder has no active offer.

Permanent Rate Promotions vs. Market

Only one builder-advertised offer in today's sweep meets the bar for a genuine permanent, life-of-loan rate promotion rather than a temporary buydown or adjustable-rate mortgage: Stylecraft Homes' 5.25% 30-year fixed FHA rate, flagged for a 4th consecutive run and still unverified directly with the builder. Every other rate-related offer swept today is either a temporary buydown that steps up after an initial period, an ARM that adjusts after a fixed window, or a short-dated sales-event rate tied to a specific signing deadline.

Buyer-relevant news: The planned 921-home Rocking Wilco subdivision in Georgetown, built by Onx Homes, is headed to a foreclosure auction after construction halted roughly nine months ago with dozens of homes left unfinished. It's a stark reminder that a builder's financial stability and completed track record matter as much as price or incentives — ask to see finished communities in person before signing, and understand how your earnest money is protected if construction stalls.
