When a builder's own page disagrees with itself from one day to the next

Most of what we flag as a "coverage gap" is a page that genuinely didn't render the same way twice — a script-gated offer modal, a truncated fetch, a layout change. Today's Ashton Woods page is a different and more useful example: the same community (Waldsanger) and the same stale 2023 footnote text appears to have been read as disqualifying one day and not the next, while a separate sitewide banner flipped the opposite direction. That's not evidence the offers actually changed — it's a reminder that "expired" and "live" sometimes come down to a judgment call about fine print that a human would resolve in seconds by reading the page directly.

The practical lesson for buyers is the same one we give every time a standing offer seems to vanish or reappear: don't treat any one day's snapshot — ours or the builder's own page — as the final word. If a sales rep quotes you a number that doesn't match what you see here, ask them to point to the specific page and date, and get the current terms in writing before you rely on them.

Giddens Homes' disappearance today is the cleaner case: no ambiguous footnote, just a homepage that used to list two offers and today lists none. That's the kind of change we're comfortable calling a real removal — the distinction is whether the page states something clearly (gone) or whether it's genuinely unreadable (gap).

New construction is now cheaper than resale in a growing number of markets, reporting finds

Recent reporting highlights a structural shift: the long-standing price premium new homes have historically carried over comparable resale listings has narrowed or inverted in a number of U.S. metros, driven by builders' sustained use of rate buydowns, price cuts, and closing-cost credits to move inventory. (FOX 7 Austin, published Oct 4, 2026 — now in its 11th day in this slot; we checked one fresher candidate this run, a Community Impact piece on Austin-area home sales that turned out to be dated November 2023 despite surfacing in a 2026 search, so it stays.)

For buyers: this is exactly the mechanism we track here every day. Austin-area builders are carrying a median advertised incentive in the low-$20,000s across 33 active builders today, with several specific communities exceeding $80,000–$105,000. Before assuming a resale listing is the cheaper option, get the builder's current incentive-adjusted price in writing and run the full comparison — price, incentive value, HOA, warranty, and any lender-tied rate terms — rather than comparing list prices alone.
