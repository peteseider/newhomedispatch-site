Permanent Rate Promotions vs. Market

No permanent-rate promotions are confirmed live today — 7th consecutive run at zero. We report a permanent-rate promotion only when a builder's own page or paperwork uses language like "permanent" or "life of the loan," and we say so plainly when that bar isn't met. Several builders (D.R. Horton, Chesmar Homes, Toll Brothers, Regency by Toll Brothers) advertise 30-year fixed rates in the mid-to-high 4s and 5s, but none explicitly discloses the rate as permanent, so none is counted here.

Freddie Mac PMMS 30-year benchmark: 7.32%, new print this week (down from 7.40%). A $400,000 loan at 7.32% vs. a builder's advertised 4.99%–5.50% rate runs roughly $400–$550 less per month while the lower rate is in effect — always confirm in writing whether a builder-advertised rate is temporary, an ARM, or tied to a required affiliate lender before treating it as your long-term payment.

Buyer News

Why Buying a New Home Is Now Cheaper Than an Existing One in Many Markets — FOX 7 Austin. Market data shows the long-standing new-construction price premium over resale has narrowed or inverted in several metros, including Austin, as builders lean on rate buydowns, price cuts, and closing-cost credits. Buyer takeaway: before assuming a resale home is cheaper, get the builder's current incentive-adjusted price in writing and compare total cost, not just list price.
