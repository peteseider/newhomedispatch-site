Why a disappearing standing offer doesn't automatically mean it's gone for good

Builder incentive pages change their structure constantly — a rate offer that's been on a page for weeks can vanish from one day's fetch simply because the page layout shifted, a promo module failed to render, or the builder temporarily pulled it down for a refresh. That's different from a builder explicitly stating an offer has ended, the way M/I Homes' page now reads "No Incentives are currently scheduled for this market."

That distinction matters for how you should act on it. If a sales rep tells you a rate special you saw advertised is "still available" even though it's not on the page today, that's worth confirming in writing before you rely on it — and it's equally worth asking whether it was ever a true permanent rate to begin with, or a temporary buydown or ARM dressed up in simpler language. D.R. Horton's disappeared 4.99% claim today is a good example: it was described as a full-term fixed rate on two community pages for several runs running, but we never had the underlying loan disclosure in hand, only the builder's own marketing language.

Today's Regency by Toll Brothers footnote is the opposite case — new information that still doesn't answer the core question. It names specific underwriting terms (740 credit score, 10% down, a defined loan range) but still never uses the word "permanent." Before assuming any advertised rate is fixed for the life of your loan, ask the builder's lender directly for the loan type in writing — fixed, ARM, or step-up buydown — not just the headline number.

New construction is now cheaper than resale in a growing number of markets, reporting finds

Recent reporting highlights a structural shift: the long-standing price premium new homes have historically carried over comparable resale listings has narrowed or inverted in a number of U.S. metros, driven by builders' sustained use of rate buydowns, price cuts, and closing-cost credits to move inventory. (FOX 7 Austin, published Oct 4, 2026 — now in its 10th day in this slot; we checked one fresher candidate this run, a Community Impact piece on a builder expanding into Austin-area communities that turned out to be dated 2023 despite surfacing in a 2026 search, so it stays.)

For buyers: this is exactly the mechanism we track here every day. Austin-area builders are carrying a median advertised incentive around $20,000 across 34 active builders today, with several specific communities exceeding $80,000–$105,000. Before assuming a resale listing is the cheaper option, get the builder's current incentive-adjusted price in writing and run the full comparison — price, incentive value, HOA, warranty, and any lender-tied rate terms — rather than comparing list prices alone.
