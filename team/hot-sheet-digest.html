What a permanent rate buydown actually costs — and how to use that

A permanent below-market rate isn't free. To hold, say, 4.99% when the market is 6.66%, the builder pre-pays the lender a forward-commitment fee that commonly runs about 4–6% of the loan amount — roughly $18,000–$27,000 on a $450,000 loan. That is real builder money, and it is the same pool of money a builder could otherwise hand you as a price cut or closing-cost credit.

So the rate is a choice, not a gift. If you plan to keep the loan a long time, the permanent buydown usually wins. But if you're paying cash, expect to move or refinance within a few years, or simply value price over payment, ask the builder to reallocate that buydown cost to the sale price or to closing costs instead — several builders here (Perry, Stylecraft, Tri Pointe) already frame it as "rate OR cash, your choice."

With the big month-end cluster closing tomorrow, don't assume a lapsed offer means a worse deal is coming — ask directly whether the builder is relaunching with a new campaign or simply pulling back. And as always: confirm any rate or credit is still inside its stated window with a sales rep before you rely on the webpage alone, in either direction.

New construction is underpricing resale in Austin Metro right now

Spectrum News 1 Texas reports Austin-area buyers are increasingly choosing new construction over resale as builders lean on incentives to move inventory. The median price for newly built homes in Austin Metro was about $411,000 in July, versus $435,000 for all homes listed area-wide — new construction is running roughly $24,000 cheaper at the median. Some communities are advertising first-year mortgage rates as low as 3.25% against a prevailing market rate around 6.625%, plus design credits and closing-cost assistance. Austin realtor Justyn LeFebvre is quoted saying resale sellers increasingly have to price "more in line with where the builders are building." (Spectrum News 1 Texas, Aug 12, 2026 — carried forward; last re-checked Aug 30, when an 8-angle search pass found no fresher qualifying story. This story is now 12 days past its own Aug 18 review deadline — an open editorial decision noted in the delta record.)

For buyers: that $24,000 median gap is a structural condition right now, not a one-off promotion — if you're cross-shopping new vs. resale, compare total cost (price minus incentive value, including the true cost of a temporary buydown) rather than list price alone, and ask every builder whether an advertised first-year rate is permanent or reverts after year one — and whether the offer is still inside its printed contract window.
