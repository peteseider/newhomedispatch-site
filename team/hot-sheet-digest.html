What a permanent rate buydown actually costs — and how to use that

A permanent below-market rate isn't free. To hold, say, 3.99% when the market is 6.71%, the builder pre-pays the lender a forward-commitment fee that commonly runs about 4–6% of the loan amount — roughly $18,000–$27,000 on a $450,000 loan. That is real builder money, and it is the same pool of money a builder could otherwise hand you as a price cut or closing-cost credit.

So the rate is a choice, not a gift. If you plan to keep the loan a long time, the permanent buydown usually wins. But if you're paying cash, expect to move or refinance within a few years, or simply value price over payment, ask the builder to reallocate that buydown cost to the sale price or to closing costs instead.

Today's benchmark move is a small reminder of why "as low as X%" claims need a same-day comparison point: a rate that looked modest against 6.66% yesterday looks slightly better against 6.71% today, even though nothing about the builder's offer changed. Always check the offer's rate against the day's actual market rate, not last week's number.

Kyle and San Marcos: three road projects underway on the growth corridor

Community Impact rounds up three active Hays County road projects: a $19.9-21.6M widening of Center Street in Kyle (sidewalks, lighting, a roundabout; construction began July 13, 2026, running through summer 2027), a $3.4M Sessom Creek slope stabilization at Canyon Road in San Marcos, and a $10.2M Canyon Road paving project (fall 2026-fall 2027). Much of the funding traces to the 2022 Kyle Road Bond. (Community Impact, published Aug 27, 2026.)

For buyers: both Kyle and San Marcos sit inside the corridor this sweep tracks closely. Expect construction-related traffic and access disruptions near Center Street and Canyon Road into 2027 — but the bond-funded investment signals continued public support for road capacity in the corridor. If you're evaluating a community near either project, ask the builder or the county for the current construction timeline and detour routes before assuming smooth access.
