Permanent Rate Promotions vs. Market

No permanent-rate promotions are confirmed live today — 6th consecutive run at zero. We report a permanent-rate promotion only when a builder's own page or paperwork uses language like "permanent" or "life of the loan," and we say so plainly when that bar isn't met. Regency by Toll Brothers' and Toll Brothers' 5.49% rates both remain withheld from this list, consistent with our direct check that found the Regency rate explicitly time-boxed to a signing window rather than permanent.

Freddie Mac PMMS 30-year benchmark: 7.40% as of the week of Oct 15 (held, 8th consecutive reading). A $400,000 loan at 7.40% vs. a builder's advertised 5.49% runs roughly $450–$500 less per month while the lower rate is in effect — always confirm in writing whether a builder-advertised rate is temporary, an ARM, or tied to a required affiliate lender before treating it as your long-term payment.

Why Buying a New Home Is Now Cheaper Than an Existing One in Many Markets — FOX 7 Austin. Market data shows the long-standing new-construction price premium over resale has narrowed or inverted in several metros, including Austin, as builders lean on rate buydowns, price cuts, and closing-cost credits. Buyer takeaway: before assuming a resale home is cheaper, get the builder's current incentive-adjusted price in writing and compare total cost, not just list price.
