What a permanent rate buydown actually costs — and how to use that

A permanent below-market rate isn't free. To hold, say, 3.99% when the market is 6.66%, the builder pre-pays the lender a forward-commitment fee that commonly runs about 4–6% of the loan amount — roughly $18,000–$27,000 on a $450,000 loan. That is real builder money, and it is the same pool of money a builder could otherwise hand you as a price cut or closing-cost credit.

So the rate is a choice, not a gift. If you plan to keep the loan a long time, the permanent buydown usually wins. But if you're paying cash, expect to move or refinance within a few years, or simply value price over payment, ask the builder to reallocate that buydown cost to the sale price or to closing costs instead.

Pacesetter Homes reopening its offer under a brand-new contract window — one day after the old one lapsed — is a reminder that these deadlines are real operating levers for the builder, not just marketing copy. A window closing doesn't necessarily mean the incentive is gone for good; it means the terms reset, and it's worth asking a sales rep what the new window actually requires.

Hays County lands $39M for a railroad overpass on the Kyle growth corridor

The Federal Railroad Administration awarded Hays County a $39.23 million grant — 80% of project cost — to build a grade-separated overpass over the Union Pacific crossing at Centerpoint Road, running from I-35 to FM 2439/Hunter Road. Hays County and Union Pacific will cover the remaining ~$9.81 million. Preliminary engineering and schematic design are done; right-of-way acquisition and final design are underway, with no construction start date given yet. (Community Impact, published Sept 1, 2026; re-checked today — a Kyle budget-shortfall story, a San Marcos CISD leadership story, and a Kyle roundabout-funding story all surfaced but none tie to new construction as directly, so no fresher qualifying story was found.)

For buyers: Centerpoint Road sits in the Kyle/Hays County growth corridor this sweep tracks closely. This is new public investment in the corridor's road capacity and safety, but it's still in the design/right-of-way phase, so it won't shorten today's at-grade crossing waits. If you're evaluating a community near Centerpoint Road, I-35, or FM 2439, ask the builder or the county for the project's expected construction timeline before assuming near-term relief.
