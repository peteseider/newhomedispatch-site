What a permanent rate buydown actually costs — and how to use that

A permanent below-market rate isn't free. To hold, say, 3.99% when the market is 6.76%, the builder pre-pays the lender a forward-commitment fee that commonly runs about 4–6% of the loan amount — roughly $18,000–$27,000 on a $450,000 loan. That is real builder money, and it is the same pool of money a builder could otherwise hand you as a price cut or closing-cost credit.

So the rate is a choice, not a gift. If you plan to keep the loan a long time, the permanent buydown usually wins. But if you're paying cash, expect to move or refinance within a few years, or simply value price over payment, ask the builder to reallocate that buydown cost to the sale price or to closing costs instead.

Today's newly-expired Meritage Homes offer is a useful reminder from the other direction: a 3.99% five-year fixed rate is not a permanent buydown — it resets after year five, and its value depends entirely on how long you plan to hold the loan past that point. Always ask what a "fixed" rate is fixed for.

For buyers: both Kyle and San Marcos sit inside the corridor this sweep tracks closely. If you're evaluating a community near either project, ask the builder or the county for the current construction timeline and detour routes before assuming smooth access.
