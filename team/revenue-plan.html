01 · The target, reverse-engineered

Working backward from $400,000

A revenue goal is not a plan. It becomes a plan the moment it is broken into the number of closings required, then the number of contracts, appointments, and leads required to produce those closings. Everything below is a model, not a forecast, built so the real numbers can be dropped in as they firm up.

Assumptions used for the worked example below (adjust to your real numbers and this recalculates itself): average sale price across the deal mix $475,000 · average commission per side 2.75% ($13,063 gross) · illustrative net-to-Pete after brokerage split and team costs, 70% ($9,144 net per closing). The brokerage-split percentage is the one number in this model that should be replaced with LPT Realty / Ascend Group's actual terms; 70% is a placeholder for the shape of the math, not a claim about the real split.
Target

$400,000 GCI

÷ $9,144 net per closing (illustrative) = ≈ 44 closings in 12 months, ≈ 3.7 / month

Contracts needed

Accounting for fall-through

44 closings ÷ (1 − 12% typical fall-through) = ≈ 50 contracts written over the year

Real appointments needed

Buyer consultations & listing appointments

50 contracts ÷ 30–35% appointment-to-contract conversion = ≈ 150–165 real appointments

Qualified leads / contacts needed

Blended across all four sources

150–165 appointments ÷ ~20% blended lead-to-appointment rate = ≈ 750–825 qualified conversations a year, ≈ 62–69 / month, ≈ 15 / week

The blended 20% conversion rate hides the real story: it is an average of four very different sources, and the entire point of the three-prong funnel in the Flow Book (New Home Dispatch → peteseider.com → Seider Realty Team) is to shift the mix toward the sources that convert far above 20%, not to try to force a cold source to perform like a warm one.

Database / past clients40–60%Highest trust source in the funnel. Under-used between transactions is the most common way this number gets left on the table.

Read against the 44-closing target: a database and referral-heavy mix needs far fewer total conversations than a Zillow-heavy one to hit the same number. The database strategy below exists because it is the cheapest, highest-converting lever in this whole model, and it is the one most agents let go cold.

02 · Database strategy

The highest-leverage lever in the plan

Roughly 400 past clients and contacts already exist in the database, per the Flow Book's funnel diagram. At a 40–60% lead-to-appointment rate, systematically working this list outperforms any amount of new cold-lead volume. The database is not a list to email once a quarter, it is the plan's foundation.

03 · The 12-month roadmap

Four quarters, one pace

44 closings a year is not an even 3.7 a month in practice, Central Texas has real seasonality: spring and early summer carry more transaction volume than the winter months. The quarterly targets below front-load the calendar year's natural buying season rather than pretending every month is identical.

Q1 · Jan–Mar

Foundation
  • Full database reactivation pass, every contact touched at least once
  • Follow Up Boss hygiene audit, close every gap the Flow Book's Lead & CRM section flags as "detail coming"
  • Zillow response-time protocol formalized and drilled, see the Zillow Conversion System
  • Target: 8–9 closings

Q2 · Apr–Jun

Ramp
  • Peak spring buying season, heaviest field and tour volume of the year
  • Referral-ask campaign to Q1's closed clients
  • Content and Dispatch cadence increased to match seasonal search volume
  • Target: 12–13 closings

Q3 · Jul–Sep

Correct
  • Mid-year KPI review against the $400K pace, see below
  • Rebalance lead-source mix using actual H1 conversion data, not the modeled assumptions
  • New-construction closing wave, builder incentive and QMI season
  • Target: 11 closings

Q4 · Oct–Dec

Close & seed
  • Push every open contract to close before year-end
  • Holiday-season story collection and referral campaign, per the Flow Book's post-close scripts
  • Seed next year's database and set the following year's target from real, not modeled, conversion rates
  • Target: 11–12 closings
04 · Review cadence

The plan corrects itself before the year does

A model built once in January and never checked again is a guess with extra steps. Every cadence below feeds the next one up.

Daily

Activity

  • 8–12 outreach touches logged
  • Every new lead entered into Follow Up Boss same day, no exceptions
  • Zillow response-time compliance: live answer or callback inside 5 minutes
Weekly

Pipeline

  • Count of leads at each Buyer Lifecycle Engine stage
  • Appointments set vs. the ≈15/week pace needed
  • Weekly Dispatch open and click performance, the top of the funnel
Monthly

Conversion

  • Closings vs. the ≈3.7/month pace
  • Lead-source ROI: which source actually produced the appointments and closings this month
  • CRM hygiene: no lead sitting untouched past its cadence window
Quarterly

Plan vs. reality

  • GCI-to-date vs. the $400K pace, with a named catch-up plan if behind
  • Database growth rate and referral rate from the quarter's closings
  • Recalculate the funnel with this quarter's real conversion rates, replace the modeled assumptions
If a quarter comes in under pace: the fix is almost never "work harder," it is "check which stage of the funnel actually broke." A miss at the lead stage means the source mix needs to shift toward the database and referrals. A miss at the appointment stage means the nurture and follow-up discipline in stage 4 of the Buyer Lifecycle Engine needs a hard look. A miss at the contract stage is a negotiation or positioning issue, not a volume issue. Diagnose before adding activity.
