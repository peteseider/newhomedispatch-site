The Zillow Conversion System

Zillow is the highest-volume, lowest-trust lead source in the whole funnel. This is the deep operating system for it: how Zillow's business actually works, why response speed decides almost everything, the psychology of a buyer who has never heard of Pete, and a full decision tree, objection map, and CRM protocol for each of the three lead types Zillow sends.

01 · The business model

Understand what Zillow is actually selling

Zillow is not a referral service that shares in a closing's success. It is an advertising platform. Premier Agent sells visibility and lead volume in a given zip code, paid up front or on a per-lead basis, regardless of what happens after the lead lands. That single fact explains almost every dynamic below.

Volume,
not outcome
Zillow is paid whether or not a lead ever closes. It has no direct financial stake in lead quality, only in lead volume and agent activity on the platform.
Often
shared
Outside exclusive markets, the same buyer inquiry can be routed to more than one Premier Agent at once. The first agent to respond well usually wins the relationship.
Response
rewarded
Zillow's own agent-ranking and profile placement weigh response rate, review volume, and activity. Answering fast and well literally buys better future lead flow.

The practical read: nobody on Zillow's side is filtering these leads for how ready or serious they are. That filtering job belongs entirely to the agent, on the call. Treating every Zillow lead identically, whether it is someone months from being ready or someone ready to write an offer this week, wastes the discipline this system is built to create.

02 · Buyer psychology on a cold, portal-driven channel

Why the first five minutes matter more than the next five calls

A Zillow lead starts at zero trust. They do not know Pete, they are very likely looking at multiple listings and possibly multiple agents at once, and their default assumption is that whoever answers is "an agent," interchangeable with any other. Four behavioral patterns explain almost everything that works in the playbooks below.

PatternWhat it means here
AnchoringThe first agent who gives a specific, credible, useful answer becomes the reference point the buyer measures every other agent against for the rest of the search. Being first with something real beats being polished but slow.
ReciprocityHanding over something free and genuinely useful immediately, a New Home Dispatch tool, a real answer to their exact question, primes the buyer to reciprocate with engagement and, eventually, trust. This is the entire reason the funnel routes Zillow leads to NHD before ever pitching representation.
Social proofOn a channel where the buyer has zero personal history with the agent, third-party proof, reviews, the Top Agent badge, does disproportionately more trust-building work than it would with a warm referral, who already trusts the person who sent them.
Loss aversion / real urgencyIn a genuinely competitive listing situation, buyers respond to real urgency, an actual second showing scheduled, an actual other offer. Manufactured urgency is detectable and destroys the trust this whole system depends on. Never fabricate it.
The research behind the 5-minute rule: lead response management research (the widely cited MIT / InsideSales.com studies, popularized by Harvard Business Review) found that contacting a lead within 5 minutes, versus waiting 30, made a rep dramatically more likely to ever reach that person at all, commonly cited at roughly 100 times more likely, and roughly 21 times more likely to qualify the lead once reached. The exact multiple varies by study and industry, but the direction and the order of magnitude are consistent across nearly every version of this research: speed is not a nice-to-have, it is close to the entire game on a cold channel. See sources at the bottom of this page.
03 · Three lead types, three playbooks

Zillow does not send one kind of lead

Phone Call, Tour Request, and Request More Information are three different buyers at three different points in their decision, and treating them identically is the most common way a good lead goes cold. Each playbook below has its own decision tree, script direction, objection map, CRM protocol, and the metric that actually measures whether it is working.

Playbook 1 of 3 · Highest intent, lowest volume

Phone Call Leads

Zillow routes an inbound call straight to Pete's phone the moment a buyer taps "call" on a listing. This is the highest-intent, lowest-volume Zillow lead type, and the one where speed matters in literal seconds, not minutes.

Decision tree

Call comes in from a Zillow listing
↓
Answered live within 3 rings?
YES → run the qualify-five-questions script (Buyer Lifecycle Engine, stage 3) live, on this call
↓ branch on their answer
"Actively touring, ready soon" → book a showing or strategy call before hanging up, log stage = Ready
"Early research, just starting" → send one NHD tool matched to what they asked about, add to Dispatch, log stage = Nurture
"Just browsing, no timeline" → warm exit, NHD link only, no hard pitch, log stage = Long-term nurture
NO, call missed → callback attempt inside 5 minutes, every time, no exceptions
Reached on callback?
YES → same qualify script as above
NO → text within 5 minutes of the missed call, then enter an 8-touch-over-8-day cadence, alternating call, text, and email, before moving to long-term nurture

Script direction

"Hi, this is Pete Seider, thanks for reaching out through Zillow. I saw you were looking at [address or community], is this still something you are actively looking at, or just starting to explore?" — full version in the Buyer Lifecycle Engine, stage 2.

Objection map

They sayRead & response
"I'm just looking"Believe them. Do not push. Send one NHD tool, add to the Dispatch list, close the call warmly. Pushing here burns trust that could convert months later.
"I'm already working with an agent"Professional, immediate, gracious exit. Log as info-only, no further pursuit. This protects both the relationship and the ethics of not working another agent's client.
"Can I just get the listing agent's number"They likely think Pete is the listing agent. Clarify the buyer's-agent role in one sentence, note it is typically no cost to them, then pivot to what representation actually gets them.

CRM protocol

Log the outcome and stage in Follow Up Boss the same day, tag source = Zillow-Call, set the next-touch reminder per the branch reached above.

Success metrics

% of Zillow calls answered live · average callback time on missed calls (target under 5 minutes) · call-to-appointment conversion rate, tracked monthly against the Revenue Plan's funnel targets.

Playbook 2 of 3 · High intent, one specific home

Tour Request Leads

A tour request means the buyer picked an actual home and asked to see it, the highest-intent signal Zillow sends short of a direct call. The job here is to confirm fast and immediately show them Pete knows more than the one listing they clicked.

Decision tree

Tour request submitted on a specific listing
↓
Confirm the appointment inside 1 hour
↓ same message, always both
Send 2–3 comparable or similar listings alongside the confirmation, every time
↓
Is this an NHD-uncovered community, or one due for a refresh?
YES → run the Rapid Field Capture on the same trip, per the Flow Book's Field Tools section, two birds one trip
NO → proceed straight to the pre-tour text below
↓
Pre-tour text: send the Look Closer 25-point checklist the day of the tour
↓
At the tour: run the model-home script from the Buyer Lifecycle Engine, offer & negotiate stage
↓
Same-day post-tour feedback call or text
Wants to see more homes → book the next tour before ending the call, log stage = Actively touring
Not the right fit → log feedback, keep on the comp-set nurture list, do not chase

Why the comp set matters more than the confirmation

The one listing they clicked may go under contract, be overpriced for the area, or simply not be the right fit once they see it. Sending 2–3 comparable homes alongside every confirmation, unprompted, is what separates a door-opener from an advisor on the very first message a Zillow tour lead receives from Pete.

Objection map

They sayRead & response
"I just want to see this one house"Respect it, do not push the comp set a second time. Mention it once in the confirmation and let it sit.
"I found this myself, I don't need an agent"Do not argue. Explain buyer's-agent value once, briefly, negotiation, inspection guidance, contract protection, then let the free tools and the tour itself do the trust-building instead of a repeated pitch.

CRM protocol

Log the specific address and the comp set sent, tag source = Zillow-Tour, schedule the post-tour feedback touch automatically.

Success metrics

Tour-confirmation speed · tour-to-second-appointment conversion (did they want to see more homes with Pete afterward) · tour-to-questionnaire conversion rate.

Playbook 3 of 3 · Lowest friction, highest volume

Request More Information Leads

This is the lowest-commitment, highest-volume Zillow lead type: someone wants more photos, school info, HOA details, or a straight answer to one question. Most are early. The discipline here is different from the other two, the real risk is under-responding, not over-pushing.

Decision tree

"Request more information" submitted
↓
Same-day reply that answers the specific question actually asked, not a generic pitch
↓
Include one NHD or true-cost tool matched to the question, per the Nurture & Educate send-table
↓
Do they reply or engage with the tool within 3 days?
YES → escalate to a phone qualify call, treat as a warmed lead from here, same tree as Playbook 1
NO → move into the Dispatch / long-term nurture list, no aggressive follow-up on a lead that showed low intent

Why response rate matters even on leads that never convert

Zillow's own agent-ranking weighs response rate across every lead type, not just the ones that turn into appointments. Answering every single "request more information," even ones that visibly go nowhere, protects and improves the algorithmic lead flow this entire system depends on. Under-responding here has a cost that shows up weeks later, in worse future lead volume, not in this lead specifically.

Objection map

Almost none needed. The risk profile of this lead type is entirely different from the other two: nobody is annoyed by a fast, specific, useful answer to a question they asked. The discipline is answering every one, not being careful about how hard to push.

CRM protocol

Log every request, including non-responsive ones, tag source = Zillow-Info. This protects the response-rate metric even when a lead never converts.

Success metrics

Response rate (target near 100%, since it directly feeds Zillow's lead-routing algorithm) · response time · reply-to-your-answer rate · eventual appointment conversion (expect the lowest rate of the three playbooks, and the highest raw volume).

04 · How this ties back to the funnel

Zillow is the cold start, not the whole system

Every playbook above routes toward the same place: New Home Dispatch first, to build trust with someone who has none yet, then peteseider.com to convert that trust into a named lead, then Seider Realty Team to close it. Nothing in this document replaces the Buyer Lifecycle Engine already in the Flow Book, it is the deep, source-specific version of stage 1 through 3 for the coldest, highest-volume source in the whole funnel. The response-time and CRM disciplines here feed directly into the Revenue Plan's funnel math: a Zillow lead-to-appointment rate that moves from 8% to 15% because of faster response and better routing has a larger effect on the $400,000 target than almost any other single lever in this plan.
